## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH PITCH

Modern-day Egypt. An archaeologist named **Dr. Miriam Hale** digs a 1,600-year-old leather
book out of a sealed shaft at Giza, and then she vanishes. The book is **the Codex**. It was
written in 391 AD by a priest-librarian who saved the most dangerous scrolls of the Library
of Alexandria the night the Serapeum burned, hid them across Egypt, and wrote this book as
the only key. Everyone wants it: a billionaire collector, Cairo's biggest antiquities
smuggler, a corrupt police colonel, and a secret society of priests who were never supposed
to still exist.

You're pulled in from one of four backgrounds, and you follow the Codex across Egypt: from
Cairo's rooftops to the sunken harbor of Alexandria, the Western Desert, the Valley of the
Kings, Nubia and Sinai, and finally back beneath Giza.

Most of the way it's a grounded treasure-hunt thriller. But one of the hidden scrolls is
the one the priests were most afraid of: **the Book of Thoth**. If you choose to open it,
the story turns into something darker.

## 2. THE REAL HISTORY IT'S BUILT ON

The fiction sits on top of real history. Everything below is real unless marked (fiction).

| Real fact | How the story uses it |
|---|---|
| **391 AD:** Christian mobs destroy the Serapeum, the "daughter library" of Alexandria. | The night the Codex's author saves the scrolls. |
| **394 AD:** the last hieroglyphic inscription ever carved, at Philae (the *Graffito of Esmet-Akhom*). | (Fiction:) Esmet-Akhom helped hide the scrolls. Philae is a key late-game location. |
| **The Setne Khamwas tales:** real ancient Egyptian stories about Prince Khaemwaset (son of Ramesses II, "the first Egyptologist"), who steals the cursed Book of Thoth from a tomb at Memphis and pays for it. | The Book of Thoth legend at the center of the story. The tale's curse (the reader's family is taken) becomes the supernatural path's rule. |
| **Hermopolis (Khemenu)** was Thoth's real cult city. Its necropolis Tuna el-Gebel holds millions of mummified ibises. | A major mid-game region. |
| **The Osiris Shaft** at Giza: a real multi-level shaft beneath the causeway, excavated in 1999. | Where Miriam found the Codex, and the final region. |
| **Alexander the Great's tomb** has been lost since antiquity. There have been real claims it's at Siwa. | A huge optional discovery in Siwa. |
| **The Lost Army of Cambyses** (50,000 Persian soldiers swallowed by a sandstorm, 524 BC). | A Western Desert side-quest chain. |
| **The 1881 Deir el-Bahari royal cache:** the Abd el-Rassul family of Qurna secretly sold pharaohs' treasures for a decade. | Backstory of the smuggling family you deal with in Luxor. |
| **Codex Sinaiticus** and St. Catherine's Monastery, one of the oldest working libraries on earth. | The Sinai region and the Coptic/Greek scholarship thread. |
| **Serabit el-Khadim** turquoise mines, the birthplace of the first alphabet (Proto-Sinaitic). | The final cipher key. |
| **The Nubian displacement:** Nubian villages drowned by Lake Nasser in the 1960s, and Abu Simbel moved block by block. | The emotional heart of the Nubian chapters. |
| **The modern antiquities-trafficking trade** through freeports and "cultural foundations." | The collector villain's operation. |

## 3. THE CODEX (what it actually is)

- **Author (fiction):** **Petamun, called Philon**, an Egyptian priest-librarian of the Serapeum.
- **Contents:** it's written in Greek and Coptic, with hieroglyphic cipher-marks that only
  the last priests could read. It indexes **seven "Houses"**, the hiding places of the
  saved scrolls. Each House holds scrolls plus the key to the next one.
- **The prize:** the **Lesser Library**, hundreds of scrolls thought lost with Alexandria
  (lost plays, maps, histories and science). Grounded, and priceless.
- **The danger:** among them is a sealed box Petamun refused to catalogue. It's the scroll
  the priests believed was the **Book of Thoth**, the same one Khaemwaset stole and gave
  back 1,600 years earlier.
- **Is the Book real?** That's up to the player. If you never open it, it's never proven.
  The weird things stay deniable (coincidence, heat, exhaustion). If you do open it, the
  game stops pretending. This is how "mostly thriller, can turn into horror" works.

## 4. THE PLAYER — FOUR BACKGROUNDS

You choose your **name, gender and background** at the start.

Each background has its **own opening region** that the others never see. You have to
replay to experience them. All four converge in **Cairo (Chapter 2)**, and from then on
it's one shared storyline. Your background still changes:

- Your **starting relationships**: who already trusts you or hates you.
- Your **starting languages and skills**.
- **Unique dialogue options** all game, and a few background-only side quests.
- Small variations in some endings.

| Background | Opening region (Ch1) | Why you're in Egypt | Starts with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Archaeologist** (foreign) | **Giza dig camp**: the Chapter 1 we've built, reworked | Hired to replace Miriam Hale, the dig director who "left suddenly." | English, basic hieroglyphs, excavation skill. The Ministry likes you, and smugglers see you as a target. |
| **The Inspector** (Egyptian) | **Saqqara**: a Ministry of Antiquities inspectorate at the necropolis of Memphis | A junior antiquities inspector assigned to a theft report nobody wants investigated: an artifact Miriam logged, then "lost." | Arabic (spoken and read), basic hieroglyphs, and police respect. Smugglers distrust you. |
| **The Fixer** (Egyptian or foreign, your pick) | **Red Sea coast**: a smugglers' harbor near Safaga | You owe money to the wrong man. The job to clear it is moving one package for a foreign woman (Miriam). The package is the Codex. | Street Arabic, lockpicking and haggling. Smugglers trust you, and the police have a file on you. |
| **The Journalist** (foreign) | **Port Said**: the Suez Canal port | Miriam emailed you three days before she vanished: *"If I stop answering, follow the Vasse shipments."* | English and French, photography, a press card that opens doors, and investigation skill. |

## 6. FACTIONS (reputation system)

Reputation mostly unlocks and locks **places, prices, safehouses, armories, quests and
NPCs**. Only one or two factions affect endings.

| Faction | Unlocks when liked | When hated |
|---|---|---|
| **Ministry & Tourist Police** | Permits, legal digs, the police armory, bail discounts | Checkpoints stop you, arrests are more likely |
| **The Gebali network** (smugglers) | Black market, fences, the Qurna safehouses, smuggling jobs | Muggings, bounty hunters |
| **The Vasse Foundation** | Money, top gear, a private jet in the late game | Hit squads during stealth sections |
| **The Keepers** | Hidden temple passages, Keeper shrines (fast travel) | Traps stay armed, some Houses are harder |
| **Bedouin tribes** | Desert guides, camels, wells, Siwa access | The desert is far deadlier |
| **Nubian community** | Nour, the Lake Nasser region, the language teacher | Nubia region restricted |

Joining one can lock another. For example, taking Vasse's money turns the Ministry cold.
**Ending impact:** only the Ministry vs. Vasse standing affects endings (who's in the room at
the end). The rest is world access.

## 8. THE MAIN STORY IN BRIEF

**Ch1 (your background).** You first touch the Codex or Miriam's trail, and someone tries
to take it from you. You leave for Cairo because you have to. Each opening ends with a
choice of how you leave (for example, the Archaeologist: sneak out with the Codex, hand it
to the Ministry and follow it, or make a deal with a stranger). This changes your Cairo
starting situation, not your ending.

**Ch2 Cairo.** The four paths converge on the same night at the Khan el-Khalili, where
Miriam had an appointment. All the factions show up. You meet Youssef, Amira, the Gebali
network and Vasse's people, and Ibrahim robs you. You learn the Codex is a map, and that
reading it takes Greek, Coptic and a cipher. Father Bishoy starts teaching you.

**Ch3 Alexandria.** House 1 lies beneath the Serapeum's foundations and the sunken harbor.
You find the first scrolls and prove the Codex is real. Vasse's security chief, Lena,
nearly beats you there.

**Ch4 The Delta.** House 2 is at Tanis. Vasse corners you, and so does the Colonel.

> **BIG CHOICE 1 — Who do you stand with?**
> **The Ministry** (legal and slow), **Vasse** (rich and fast), or **the Gebali network**
> (off the books). This decides who hunts you through Act II, which hubs are friendly,
> and who's with you in Luxor. **It does not lock endings.** You can switch sides later,
> at a cost.

**Ch5 Faiyum.** House 3 is inside the Labyrinth, and it's the biggest puzzle dungeon. The
Keepers make first contact: *"Stop. You don't know what's in the last box."*

**Ch6–7 Western Desert and Siwa.** You follow Miriam's trail into the desert and a
sandstorm nearly kills you. The Bedouin save you, or don't, depending on your standing.
At Siwa you can find Alexander's tomb, the find of the century, completely optional.

**Ch8 Hermopolis.** In the ibis galleries of Thoth's city you find **Miriam**. She's alive,
hiding with the Keepers, and not entirely herself. She opened a copy of one line from
the sealed box. You learn what the Book of Thoth is supposed to do. **Whether she's
saved, and how, is a relationship-driven thread** that pays off at the end.

**Ch9 Luxor.** House 5 is in a tomb the Qurna families have hidden for a century. The
sealed box is here, and it's the Book of Thoth.

> **BIG CHOICE 2 — Do you open the Book?**
> **Keep it sealed:** the story stays grounded (a thriller ending track).
> **Open it:** the supernatural track. The world subtly changes. You can understand what
> the ibises and the dead say, and new areas and dialogue open up. But as in the real
> ancient tale, **the Book takes from the people closest to you.** This is where
> endings start locking.

**Ch10–13 Act III.** The race south to Philae and the last priests, then east to Sinai
for the final cipher key. Vasse is at his most dangerous, and the Colonel shows which
side he's on. Lena chooses a side (depending on you), and the Keepers reveal what they
really are. On the supernatural track, the Book's cost comes due: someone close to you
is in danger, and your relationships decide who.

**Ch14 Beneath Giza.** You descend the Osiris Shaft into the Lesser Library. There's a
final stealth and escape sequence, a confrontation with Vasse, and then the last choice.

> **BIG CHOICE 3 — What happens to the Library (and the Book)?**

## 9. ENDINGS (7, each with variations)

The ending **family** comes from Big Choices 2 and 3. **Variations** within each come from
relationships, factions, Miriam's fate, who's alive, which Houses you found, and whether
you found Alexander's tomb and House 6b. It works like your burger example: same
restaurant, different order.

**Grounded track (the Book stays sealed):**

1. **The Discovery.** Reveal the Library with the Ministry. You become world-famous and
   Egypt keeps its history. *Varies by:* who stands beside you, whether Vasse goes to
   prison or walks, whether Miriam is credited.
2. **The Sale.** Sell the Library to Vasse. You're rich and in exile, and the scrolls
   vanish into freeports. *Varies by:* whether Lena lets you walk away, whether you betray
   Vasse at the last second.
3. **The Burial.** Give it back to the Keepers and reseal the Houses. Nobody ever knows.
   *Varies by:* whether you keep one scroll, and whether Miriam stays with the Keepers.

**Supernatural track (the Book was opened):**

4. **The Scribe.** Read the Book to the end. You gain everything it promised, and pay what
   the tale says. *Varies by:* who you lose, decided by relationships.
5. **The Return.** Do what Prince Khaemwaset did: return the Book to its first owner's
   tomb and make peace with the dead. The redemption ending. *Varies by:* who survives,
   and whether the Library is saved as well.
6. **The Fire.** Burn it all, the Library and the Book. Alexandria burns twice. *Varies by:*
   who helps you, and who can't forgive you.

**Secret:**

7. **The Usurper.** If Vasse gets the Book (possible if you fail certain things mid-late),
   he reads it, and the ending is from his side of the story. The only "bad" ending.

## 10. SYSTEMS THAT SHAPE THE STORY

- **Choices:** there are 3 big choices plus hundreds of small ones. The small ones change
  relationships and access, like a house you can't enter five chapters later because you
  didn't help someone in Chapter 2.
- **The "will remember this" notice:** a setting chosen at New Game (on or off).
- **Endings lock mid to late only.** Nothing in Act I can lock an ending. Most locks are
  silent, and one is obvious (Big Choice 2).
- **Failure branches instead of game over:**
  - **Robbed:** you lose cash, and a quest opens to get it back.
  - **Arrested:** jail interiors in Cairo and Luxor. Pay bail, get bribed out, or break out.
  - **Injured:** the hospital, with a bill.
  - **Death:** respawn at the last checkpoint and lose some money. Death is rarer than the
    other outcomes.
- **Light combat:** mostly stealth. Fights are rare and can usually be avoided. In big
  fights you can bring one ally.
- **Skills and XP:** languages, hieroglyphs, lockpicking, climbing, diving, haggling,
  stealth, first aid, driving and riding. Skills open dialogue, areas and shortcuts.
- **Languages as progression gates:**
  - **Egyptian Arabic:** NPCs and signs become readable as you level it.
  - **Nubian:** needed for Nour's boat.
  - **Coptic and Greek:** needed to read the Codex itself.
  - **Hieroglyphs:** needed for puzzles.
  - **Bedouin dialect:** needed for desert guides.

  You learn from tutors, by repeating jobs, and by reading. Your background decides where
  you start.
- **The Book's pull:** replaces sanity. It's hidden, and only active after you open the
  Book. It changes what you see and hear. It's not a health bar.
- **Food and water:** light. There are wells, date palms, fruit trees and street food. It
  matters mostly in the desert, and it's never a chore.
- **Day and night:** shops open and close, NPCs follow schedules, some quests are
  night-only, and tombs are easier to sneak into at night.

## 11. ECONOMY (Egyptian pounds, GTA-style)

- **What money buys:**
  - Transport (tickets, a horse, a car, boat upgrades)
  - Permits, bribes and bail
  - Hospital bills
  - Gear (detector, dive kit, climbing gear, camera, lamps, backpacks)
  - Home base upgrades
  - Language tutors
  - Information from fixers
- **Regional prices, explained in the world:**
  - Youssef jokes about the "tourist price" in Cairo.
  - Alexandria is a rich port city.
  - Desert goods are carried in by truck, so they cost more.
  - Luxor has tourist markups.
  - Nubian villages barter and are cheap.
- **Selling finds, legally or illegally:**
  - **Legally** through the Ministry: a small reward and reputation.
  - **To the Gebali fences:** good money.
  - **To Vasse:** the best money.

  Illegal sales raise police heat and cost Ministry and Keeper reputation.
- **Repeatable jobs, per region:**
  - Fishing (Lake Burullus, the Nile, the Red Sea)
  - Hauling and sieving at digs
  - Guiding tourists
  - Taxi or boat fares
  - Deliveries (by bike, horse or boat)
  - Photography commissions
  - Detector treasure-hunting
  - Carpentry and boat repair
  - Date harvesting (Siwa)
  - Diving salvage (Alexandria)
  - Smuggling runs (illegal, big money, big risk)
  - Race betting (horse and camel)
  - Backgammon (tawla) hustles in Cairo cafés
- **Home bases:**
  - Cairo rooftop room, then a Zamalek flat
  - A Luxor dahabiya houseboat, which can be upgraded and sailed
  - Optional: a Siwa mud-brick house
  - Bases store items, let you rest and save, and show your trophies.
